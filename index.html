Mapping and GIS

Site constraints and land mapping.

We combine a fresh drone survey with official mapping data to show what affects a piece of land: boundaries, protected trees, listed buildings, flood risk, paths, and pipes and cables. The result is a clear, measured plan that owners, agents and architects can all work from. The right outputs depend on the site and on the decision you need to make. Based in Gullane, we work with landowners and businesses across East Lothian and Scotland.

Site constraints map

Know what limits a site before you spend money on it.

We combine a fresh drone survey with the official datasets that matter, such as protected trees, flood risk, wildlife, archaeology, pipes and cables, and paths, and draw it all on one measured plan. You can see straight away what affects the site and how much of it is actually free to build on.

  1. Woodland belt with a tree preservation order
  2. Root protection zone, 12 m from the woodland edge
  3. Badger sett with a 30 m protection zone
  4. Surface water flood risk, medium likelihood
  5. Gas main with a 6 m build-free easement
  6. Area of archaeological interest, trial trenching
  7. Core path, to stay open
  8. Site access from the new estate road
  • Site boundary
  • National grid lines, 50 m apart
  • Hatched areas are constraints

What you get

  • An A3 constraints plan as a PDF, with a north arrow, scale bar, legend and title block
  • A table of every constraint, where the data came from and how up to date it is
  • The site area, and the area left once the constraints are taken out
  • GIS files that open in QGIS, ArcGIS or Google Earth, so your architect or agent can build on them

Ports and marine

Track progress on the water’s edge without putting anyone on the rocks.

Harbours, piers, sea walls and coastal defences are hard and risky to inspect on foot. A drone survey at low tide records the whole frontage in one go: armour placed, material stockpiled, plant on site and any movement since the last visit.

  1. Precast concrete blocks, storage and curing
  2. Crawler crane lifting zone
  3. Rock armour placed along the quay
  4. Quay infill, core material
  5. Underlayer rock, armour still to place
  6. Concrete armour units placed
  7. Haul road along the breakwater
  • Hatched areas are storage or work still to do
  • Dashed lines are indicative

What you get

  • A dated aerial record of the whole frontage on each visit
  • Progress marked by section, with lengths and areas measured
  • Stockpile volumes and armour unit counts from the same survey
  • Repeat surveys from the same flight path to spot movement over time

Useful for: marine contractors, harbour authorities, coastal engineers and councils

Peatland and environment

Map the ground in detail before restoration or development starts.

Drone mapping shows peat condition, erosion, drains and pools far more clearly than free satellite imagery. We map the site, sort it into condition classes and mark where work is needed, ready for a restoration plan, a funding application or an environmental report.

  1. Actively eroding channel banks
  2. Open water: channel, pools and burns
  3. Modified bog, damaged by grazing and vehicles
  4. Near-natural bog, in good condition
  5. Proposed peat dams to raise the water table
  6. Bare peat to re-vegetate
  7. Vehicle tracks across the bog
  8. Fixed photo points for monitoring
  • Hatched areas are condition classes
  • Dashed lines are indicative

What you get

  • A high-detail aerial map and a condition map of the whole site
  • Lengths of eroding channel and drains, and areas of bare peat, measured from the survey
  • Proposed restoration points, such as dams and reprofiling, marked and numbered
  • Repeat surveys from the same photo points, to show change for funders

Useful for: landowners, estates, environmental consultants and peatland restoration projects

Frequently asked questions

How do people view a 360° tour?

Tours open in a web browser on a phone, tablet or computer. Visitors can drag to look around, move between viewpoints and explore the property at their own pace. No app is required.

Can we add the tour to our own website?

Yes. We provide a hosted tour link and an embed code, allowing the tour to be incorporated into a compatible website. The hosted link can also be shared by email or through property listings and social media.

What happens if the weather is bad?

Weather and flying conditions are checked before each job. If conditions make the planned flight unsafe or unsuitable, we'll discuss rearranging the visit. The approach depends on the service booked and the conditions at the time.

What do we receive from a mapping project?

Deliverables depend on the requirements agreed at the outset. These may include stitched aerial imagery, mapped boundaries and features, area measurements, elevation data, 3D models and compatible GIS files. We'll confirm the outputs and their intended use before the work begins.

How far do you travel?

We're based in Gullane, East Lothian, and work across Scotland. Travel and any associated costs are confirmed in the quote, based on the location and scope of the project.
